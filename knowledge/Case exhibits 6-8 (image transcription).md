# Momentum case, Part A: Exhibits 6–8 (transcribed from the exhibit images)

## Exhibit #6: Bloomberg article, "Momentum to Buy Globex for $15 Billion in Hopes of a Forward-Thinking Strategic Move"
By Sarah Merriman, May 16, 2019.
- Momentum agreed to buy Globex for $15 billion in cash and stock, a 30% premium.
- Momentum, a global delivery company, was racing to find the best technology for the race toward autonomous vehicles. Globex is an IT-based technology firm in San Francisco, California, seen as the perfect fit to help Momentum compete with Alphabet, Amazon, Apple, Uber and others.
- Some investors initially believed Momentum overpaid for Globex; after an explanation from Momentum's CFO, the logic made sense to them.
- Autonomous vehicles can be disruptive, but no one had figured out how to make them a safe play in the industry. Momentum hoped Globex would help it release semi-autonomous delivery vehicles; if they could provide a safe way for these vehicles to exist, they could cut costs and deliver more packages efficiently.
- "It remains to be seen what the outcome will be." Advisors: Goldman Sachs (Momentum), Jefferies (Globex).

## Exhibit #8: Cost analysis of replacing drivers (traditional driver vs. technical engineer)
Average truck driver, annual: salary $56,160; benefits $16,848; bonus $10,000.
Average technical engineer (capable of monitoring 4 trucks at one time), annual: salary $120,000; benefits $36,000; bonus $50,000.
Cost-saving benefit listed: capable of monitoring 4 trucks at once = $332,032.
Arithmetic from the exhibit's figures: one driver costs $83,008 in total; four drivers cost $332,032; one engineer costs $206,000; so one engineer replacing four drivers saves $126,032 per year.
