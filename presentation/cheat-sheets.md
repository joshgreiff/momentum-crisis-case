# Board presentation cheat sheets (Fri 11/6)

15 minutes total including Board questions, so about 2 minutes each. Jenny's tips: don't summarize the case (the Board knows it), don't repeat a point someone already made, use numbers, and expect interruptions ("why did you do this?").

---

## Josh Greiff, CEO (open and close)

**Open:** three crashes, two deaths (Ashley Williams, our driver; Alexa, a student on the Pittsburgh bus), many injured. Then our principles:
1. Safety first, even at a cost
2. Tell the truth early, including the bad parts
3. Apologize without guessing the cause
4. People hear it from us first

**Key decisions to own:**
- Public apology Friday night, before we knew the cause
- Told Margie about Ashley's death that night and about IT's findings the next morning
- Sunday public statement: our software caused it, we knew about the glitch for a month, and continuing to operate was the wrong decision

**Close:** fleet stays grounded until the fix is independently tested; drivers are part of the safety review; we'll rebuild trust through actions.

**Likely questions:**
- *Why apologize before knowing the cause?* We owned the harm without guessing the cause. Speed, acknowledgment and amends are what rebuild trust, and the cause turned out to be ours.
- *Why disclose the month of prior knowledge? That's legal exposure.* It would have come out anyway (the press was already reporting "ignored signs"). Coming from us, it's accountability; coming from a reporter, it's a cover-up.
- *You're new. Why should we trust your judgment?* Every major call was made within 24 hours and stayed consistent with the same principles.

## Aman Chugh, COO

**Key points:**
- Halted all operations the day of the crashes and ordered a full headcount of drivers within the hour
- Semi-autonomous fleet grounded; deliveries continue with human drivers only, with autonomous systems off
- "To Our Drivers" (Sun): no driver is penalized for refusing a truck they think is unsafe; listening session with Emily

**Likely questions:**
- *Can we keep up with deliveries without the autonomous fleet?* Human drivers plus third-party partners if needed (Mikey's Amazon email). It costs more short term but keeps customers.
- *When do the trucks go back on the road?* Only after the fix is independently tested and the safety review (with a driver representative) signs off. No date until then.
- *How did a glitch go unreported for a month?* Concerns weren't being escalated. That's why we created a direct safety reporting line to leadership with a response within 48 hours.

## Mateo Del Toro, Globex CEO

**Key points:**
- IT confirmed the crashes came from a glitch in our own software; 95% certain there was no hack (no evidence of outside intrusion)
- IT and safety had been investigating for about a month and judged it not critical. That was the wrong call
- Replied to IT: preserve everything, support the team, keep investigating
- Next steps: root cause confirmed, fix, then independent testing before anything restarts

**Context:** earlier warning signs in the case (March 2021): L4 trucks with nonstop horns, music playing, random blinkers, sudden light braking. Drivers laughed them off, and quality checks came back "fine."

**Likely questions:**
- *Who made the call to keep running?* IT and the safety team together. We're fixing the process (escalation, independent testing), not blaming individuals.
- *Was it terrorism?* No evidence of outside intrusion so far; 95% confidence from IT.
- *Was the $15B Globex acquisition a mistake?* The technology needs stronger safety processes, not abandonment. That's why independent testing and driver input come first.

## Mikey Campbell, CMO

**Key points:**
- Pulled the commercial: about $8M spent ($1M production + $7M network fees), recovered about $0.7M (10% of fees), so roughly a $7.3M loss. Airing autonomous-truck ads during the crisis would have been tone-deaf
- Amazon: apologized for them learning from the news, then corrected the "if we need to suspend" wording. The suspension is in effect, human drivers deliver, and we're exploring third-party partners
- No marketing or social posts after the deaths

**Likely questions:**
- *Why throw away $7.3M?* Airing it would have cost far more in trust. Every headline that week was about our trucks.
- *Are we losing Amazon?* They threatened other vendors because they heard it from the news. We answered directly, corrected the record, and kept deliveries going.
- *Why did the first Amazon email say "in the event we need to suspend"?* It was written before the full suspension. We corrected it the next day so they had the real status.

## Emily Tang, CHRO

**Key points:**
- Families: condolences to the Williams family (Josh); Alexa's family reached through the school district, with funeral and medical costs covered and Emily as the one contact, no pressure to respond; grief counseling offered to the school
- Drivers and employees: all-staff message, listening session, no retaliation for speaking up
- Commitments: direct safety reporting line (response within 48 hours), driver representative on the safety review, weekly Friday updates, no layoff decisions about drivers until the investigation and safety review are complete

**Likely questions:**
- *Why not contact Alexa's family directly?* A letter from the company they blame can feel like PR. We offered support through the district and let them choose.
- *Isn't pausing layoffs just delaying the inevitable?* It's a pause, not a promise. We won't make job decisions mid-crisis without the facts, and right now drivers are running every delivery.
- *Did the walkout happen?* We answered with listening and specific safety guarantees within hours. (Update on the latest status before Friday.)

## Griffin Lewis, CFO

**Key numbers (Exhibits 6–8):**
- 2020 revenue $75.8B (about $208M a day); net income $8.1B
- Cash $2.1B vs. long-term debt $19.0B; interest about $758M a year
- Commercial: about a $7.3M loss
- Globex acquisition $15B (30% premium); almost $700M invested in the autonomous fleet
- Driver total cost about $83K a year; engineer $206K replaces four drivers, saving about $126K a year (Exhibit 8)

**Key points:**
- The crisis is survivable, but the cushion is thin: $2.1B cash against $19B of debt and unknown litigation exposure (two deaths, children critically injured, a multi-car pileup)
- The driver replacement savings depend on technology that's grounded and a law now under review, so the layoff pause is financially reasonable
- Victim support (medical and funeral) is a small cost compared with the reputation and legal damage of not doing it

**Likely questions:**
- *What's our total exposure?* Not knowable yet: litigation, insurance coverage and regulatory outcomes are pending. We'd give a range once legal and insurance weigh in.
- *Should we write down the Globex investment?* Not yet. It depends on the root cause, the fix, and what Congress does with the 2018 law.
- *Can we afford human drivers?* Yes, short term. Losing Amazon or another crash would cost far more.
