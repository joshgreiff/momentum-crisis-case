## 1. Josh (CEO): what happened and how we decided

- **What happened (one sentence only; the Board knows the case):** three crashes, two deaths, a glitch we knew about for a month, and a near walkout.
- **Principles:** people first, truth early, stakeholders hear it from us before the news, own the harm.
- **Process for every update:** the person whose role was hit drafts the response, anyone reviews it in the group chat, then we send.
- **Our app (Crisis Command Center):** we built a tool that analyzes each update from all six executive roles, using the case files and everything we had already sent, and proposes one CEO response. It also has a private Q&A for anyone on the team and a full log of what we sent and why.
- **Key message: AI suggested, we decided.** This is a central part of our story. We used the tool effectively without handing off our judgment. Every recommendation was checked against what we had already done and what each stakeholder actually needed. Examples where we went against it:
  - Walkout threat: it recommended another CEO email to all staff and new listening sessions. We skipped both because we had done them two days earlier, and repeating them would have looked like scrambling.
  - The child's death: it recommended widely publicizing family contacts and announcing workforce transition plans. Instead we reached out to the family privately through the school district and paused driver layoff decisions.
  - Management changes: it recommended starting an assessment and reporting back later. With the Board meeting two days away, that would have dodged the question, so we gave a direct answer.
- **Takeaway line:** "The tool made us faster and made sure every role's view was considered. The decisions were ours."

The risk is short-term cash ($2.1B), so we protect it: confirm the credit line, pause nonessential spending and new autonomous investment, and lean on insurance for claims.
