# Momentum crisis: decision log (Team #6, Petitto, 2:15)

For the Board presentation on Friday, Nov 6. Each entry is what we learned, what we did, and why. Full text of every email is recorded in the Crisis Command Center app under the matching update.

## How we worked

- **Every update got a response within 24 hours.** Jenny's rule; we tracked the clock and made sure at least one email went out each day.
- **Owner by role.** The executive whose function the update hit drafted the response (COO for operations, CMO for customers and marketing, CHRO for people and families, CEO for the Board and the public).
- **Group review before anything public.** Drafts went to the group chat first; anyone could flag problems. Several fixes came from this (see "Mistakes we caught").
- **AI as an advisor, not a decision-maker.** Our Crisis Command Center app ran each update past six AI executive perspectives and a consolidated recommendation, and let us ask questions about the case and everything we had sent. The team made and sent every decision.
- **Facts before claims.** We separated what was confirmed from what was assumed, and only stated causes once they were confirmed.

## Principles we kept coming back to

1. **Safety first, even at a cost.** We grounded the fleet before we knew the cause.
2. **Tell the truth early, including the bad parts.** We disclosed that the company knew about the glitch for a month before a reporter could.
3. **Apologize without guessing the cause.** Own the harm and the response; don't assign technical fault until it's confirmed.
4. **People hear it from us first.** Board chair before the press; families and employees before the public where possible.
5. **Concrete commitments over reassurance.** Funeral and medical costs, a grounded fleet until independently tested, specific employee commitments.

## Mistakes we caught (and what we learned)

- **"Seriously injured" after a death.** The apology draft and the first drivers email both called Ashley injured after she had died. We caught both in review and fixed them before sending. *Lesson:* re-check every draft against the latest update.
- **Amazon email described suspension as hypothetical.** Corrected the next day. *Lesson:* customers need the actual state, not contingencies.
- **"No vehicle on the road until 100% safe" vs. continuing deliveries.** Our early statement could have read as stopping all deliveries. Later messages clarified: semi-autonomous fleet grounded, human-driven deliveries continue.

## Likely Board questions

- *Why did you apologize before knowing the cause?* Speed, acknowledgment and amends; we owned the harm without guessing the cause, and the cause turned out to be ours.
- *Did you know about the glitch beforehand?* Yes. IT and safety had been looking into it for a month and judged it not critical. We disclosed that ourselves within about a day of learning it and called it the wrong decision.
- *What did this cost us?* About $7.3M on the commercial, revenue at risk from the grounded fleet and Amazon, and unknown litigation exposure. We have $2.1B cash against $19B of long-term debt (Exhibit 7). (Griffin's estimate.)
- *Is the autonomous strategy dead?* It's paused until the software is fixed and independently tested, with drivers involved in the safety review. The $15B Globex purchase (Exhibit 6) and roughly $700M fleet investment are at risk if the 2018 law is rolled back.
- *How did you keep the Board informed?* Margie heard about the driver's death the night it was reported, and about IT's prior knowledge the next morning, before the public statement.
- *What about the drivers?* No penalty for refusing unsafe trucks, a direct safety line, a driver on the safety review, and no layoff decisions until the review is done.
